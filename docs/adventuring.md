# Adventuring

## Damage

### Hit Points

Damage that you take reduces your current hit points (HP). When you reach 0 HP, you fall unconscious. Each point of remaining or future damage causes 1 wound.

Monsters & NPCs typically just die at 0 HP.

### Wounds

Wounds represent significant bodily injury. If you ever accumulate 10+STR wounds, you die. Your strength is relevant here because your muscle mass helps to absorb damage that would otherwise injure bones or vital organs.

You naturally heal 1 wound per long rest.

#### Wound Penalty

While you have at least one wound, you suffer a penalty equal to (wounds - CON)(min: 0) to all your d20 rolls.

* When this wound penalty reaches -4, you are also reduced to half speed.
* When it reaches -8, you can no longer stand or walk unaided.

#### Dying

When you receive a new wound, you start dying:

* You fall unconscious if you have 0 HP.
* At the end of your each of your turns, roll a d4:  
  * On a 1, you gain another wound.
  * On a 4, you become stable and are no longer dying.

#### First Aid

By taking the Help action, you can make a Medicine(INT or WIS) check to stabilize an adjacent dying creature. Having a healer's kit in your inventory can grant you advantage on this check.

### Direct Damage

Direct damage bypasses your HP and directly causes wounds. Monsters & NPCs instead take double damage to their HP from direct damage. Direct damage still counts as damage for effects that let you reduce or prevent incoming damage.

### Immunity, Resistance, and Vulnerability

* Some creatures are immune to certain kinds of damage. Damage of that type is reduced to 0 for them.
* If a creature is resistant to a type of damage, they take half the amount (rounded down, min 1).
* If they are vulnerable, they take double damage.

### Rests & Recovery

A **short rest** (usually an scene) lets you expend any number of HD (one at a time) to recover HP, as described under [Stat: Hit Dice](stats.md#hit-dice-hd).

A **long rest** (usually a night of sleep in a safe haven) lets you recover all your HP and all your expended HD. You also heal 1 wound.

## Light and Vision

### Light

In **bright light**, all characters can see normally. Bright light is produced by sunlight (which may have additional effects on some creatures) and by most other illumination sources.

An **illumination source** produces an [area](basics.md#areas) of bright light. Even creatures outside of that area can clearly see things that are illuminated within that area.

| Illumination Source | Radius | Duration |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Candle | 1 S | 1 hour |
| Lamp | 1/2 M | 8 hour per pint of oil |
| Torch | 1 M | 1 hour |
| Lantern, hooded | 1 M | 4 hours per pint of oil |
| Lantern, bullseye | 2 M cone | 4 hours per pint of oil |

If a creature cannot see through **darkness**, it is effectively blinded when interacting with anything within that darkness.

Characters may occasionally encounter areas of **dim light**, also called _shadows_ or _shadowy light_. Examples of shadowy areas include a starlit night, a phosphorescent cave, or an oubliette illuminated by a single crack of light. In an otherwise dark room, glowing embers might produce an area of dim light, or a magic weapon might dimly illuminate the tiny area occupied by it and its wielder. When characters peer beyond the area brightly illuminated by their torches, they might glimpse glowing eyes or a glint of metal in the shadows beyond.

You are not able to read, see fine detail, or discern color in dim light. Dim light imposes disadvantage on any checks that require sight, and it grants partial [concealment](combat.md#concealment). It usually takes a few rounds for your eyes to adjust to dim light. If you are standing in an area of bright light, your eyes quickly adjust to that light and most dim areas beyond the light's range becomes dark.

### Vision

Creatures with **low-light vision** treat any illumination source as having twice its radius. If they let their eyes adjust to dim light, they can see normally in it.

Creatures with **darkvision** are treated as having low-light vision while in bright or dim light. In areas of complete darkness, a creature with darkvision can see normally, except that they can only see in shades of grey. Darkvision is limited to a 2M range unless otherwise noted.

### Other Senses

A creature with **blindsight** can perceive its surroundings without relying on sight, within a specific radius.  Such sense may include sensitivity to vibrations, acute scent, keen hearing, or echolocation. A creature with blindsight cannot be blinded in the normal manner, but they might be blinded if they are deafened or otherwise deprived of the basis of their blindsight. A creature relying on blindsight is immune to gaze attacks and to visual illusions, but they cannot read or discern color or similar visual contrast.

A creature with **blindsense** has a weaker form of blindsight. They can move normally without bumping into walls, and they can locate other creatures, but those creatures still retain partial concealment.

A creature with a strong sense of **scent** can detect the presence (but not immediate location) of other creatures within 1M. This distance may be greater if the source of the smell is strong or upwind, or less if the smell is downwind or masked by another strong scent. A creature with the scent sense can spend a minor action to determine the direction to the smell, and they automatically locate the source if it is adjacent to them.  They can also track creatures over dry ground; aquatic creatures with scent may be able to track through water.

While a creature with **tremorsense** is in contact with the ground, it automatically senses the location of anything that moves within range while it is also in contact with the ground. Attacking or gesturing counts as movement for this purpose.

A creature with **truesight** can, out to a specified range, see in normal and magical darkness, see invisible creatures and objects, automatically pierce visual illusions, see into the Ethereal plane, and perceive the original form of a shapechanger or a creature that is transformed by magic.
